# EatOS must reconcile wants, needs and blocked cravings

An Indian metro resident's food life is governed by three layers that rarely agree: what they crave (identity-, memory- and context-driven), what their body and circumstances require (a high-burden metabolic and micronutrient profile), and what they cannot have (religion, family, health, cook, budget, time). The evidence supports a recommendation engine that treats these as separate data structures: **hard constraints** that filter, **soft preferences and needs** that score, and **blocked aspirations** that are stored as a wanted dish plus a blocker reason, then answered with a graded ladder of alternatives (same dish modified, near-neighbour dish, same nutritional job via another food, same dish rescheduled). Self-labelled "vegetarian" is a poor key: 39% of Indian adults call themselves vegetarian, yet 81% restrict meat in some way ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2021/06/29/religion-and-food/)), so diet must be modelled behaviourally, by day, by place (home vs outside) and by fasting calendar. The need side is dominated by diabetes (11.4% of adults, 15.3% prediabetic), salt, protein quality, anaemia and B12/vitamin D gaps, but most condition-specific numeric rules in our notes are unverified and must not ship as sourced facts. No published recommender models "wish but cannot eat", and health recommenders have not shown long-term behaviour change, so EatOS should claim reduced decision effort and variety, not disease outcomes. The proposed build is an on-device TypeScript kernel with a transparent linear scorer, a safety layer that says "unverified" instead of "safe", and a privacy posture of no account and no data leaving the phone. Evidence for many craving, culture and "wish but cannot" questions is thin; Section 6 lists every assumption made to fill those gaps. The substitution/food-data note (food_data_substitutions.md) was not present when this report was written, so substitution science and food-composition licensing remain open.

## What people want: platform data show habits, but identity and calendar set the menu

Delivery data are the only large behavioural source retrieved, and they describe online-active, higher-income users, not the whole city. Swiggy reports biryani as the top dish for the tenth year (**93M orders, 57.7M of them chicken**), ahead of burgers (44.2M), pizza (40.1M) and veg dosa (26.2M); health-focused orders (protein, low-calorie, less sugar) grew **2.3x faster** than overall orders, and midnight-to-2am orders grew about **3x faster** than dinner, led by chicken burgers, biryani, pizza, cakes and soft drinks ([Swiggy](https://www.swiggy.com/corporate/press-release/how-india-swiggyd-2025-a-feast-for-the-fast-the-foodie-and-the-future/); [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/trending/biryani-tops-indias-food-orders-again-as-swiggy-logs-93-million-plates-in-2025-86559.htm)). City texture is visible: Hyderabad leads biryani (15.7M orders in 2024) yet ranks third for vegetarian orders behind Bengaluru and Mumbai ([ETV Bharat](https://etvbharat.com/en/!bharat/biryani-tops-swiggy-2024-food-charts-with-83-million-orders-hyderabad-leads-enn24122501729); [ETV Bharat, Green Dot](https://etvbharat.com/en/!offbeat/swiggy-green-dot-award-hyderabad-3rd-position-most-vegetarian-orders-enn24080103513)), and Ramzan 2024 pushed about 6 million biryani plates, +15% over normal months ([Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/companies/swiggy-sold-close-to-6-million-biriyani-during-ramzan)). Urban household spending confirms a convenience tilt: food is about 39% of urban monthly per-capita spending, and "beverages, refreshments and processed food" is the largest food category at 10.64%, ahead of milk products at 7.22% ([MoSPI](https://mospi.gov.in/sites/default/files/press_release/Draft_Press_Brief19062024.pdf)). Platform rankings must not be read as what people eat: home-cooked, cook-made and tiffin meals are invisible in them, which is why the engine needs a "meal source" dimension (self-cooked, cook-made, ordered, canteen/tiffin).

What drives a specific craving is where evidence is weakest. Qualitative work on Indian migrants (30 interviews, in the Netherlands, not metro India) finds home food, cooking practices and commensality central to belonging ([Appetite listing](https://search.fid-benelux.de/Record/base-27602616)). We infer, without direct metro evidence, that comfort food is community- and home-region-specific (curd rice, fish curry-rice, rajma-chawal, khichdi), so EatOS should ask "home region" and "family community" explicitly rather than infer them from the city of residence. Weather, mood and stress effects on cravings are widely believed but no Indian peer-reviewed support was found; these belong as context features whose weights are learned from the user's own behaviour.

## Religion, region and fasting create the hardest filters

Diet identity is layered. Pew's survey (29,999 interviews) finds 44% of Hindus and 92% of Jains self-identify as vegetarian, 97% of Jains and about two thirds of Muslims (67%) and Christians (66%) follow some meat restriction, and 67% of Jains (about 21% of Hindus, 18% of Sikhs) avoid root vegetables ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2021/07/08/eight-in-ten-indians-limit-meat-in-their-diets-and-four-in-ten-consider-themselves-vegetarian/)). NFHS-5 shows steep state and gender gradients: weekly-or-more fish/chicken/meat is 93% of Kerala women but 4% of Punjab women, and by religion any non-veg intake is 80% of Christian men against 52.5% of Hindu men ([Sabrang summary of NFHS-5](https://sabrangindia.in/article/nfhs-5-data-busts-right-wing-myth-indian-vegetarianism/); secondary source). The 29% (SRS 2014), 39% (Pew) and NFHS figures measure different things, so the engine should use the behavioural definition: what the person eats, in which context, on which days.

Fasting needs both allow-lists and deny-lists. Navratri vrat typically allows sabudana, kuttu, rajgira, samak rice, potato, fruit, dairy, nuts and rock salt, and avoids wheat, ordinary rice, pulses, onion, garlic and table salt, but sources disagree on spices such as turmeric and hing ([Veg Recipes of India](https://www.vegrecipesofindia.com/navratri-fasting-rules-vrat-ka-khana/); [The Quint](https://www.thequint.com/fit/navratri-2022-foods-and-eat-and-avoid-during-navratri-fast)). Shravan is a deny-list month (meat, fish, egg, often onion and garlic, sometimes some root vegetables), with Goan restaurants running daily-changing Shravan thalis ([Herald Goa](https://www.heraldgoa.in/cafe/the-veggie-month-has-begun/28183)). Because practice varies by family, the rules must be user-editable templates, not hard-coded. Primary-source rules for Jain sunset eating and Paryushan, Ekadashi, Lent, roza timing and regional Hindu observances were not retrieved; the notes mark these as to-research, and the dates and rules in Section 5 are therefore templates to be verified.

Metro priors by city (Hyderabad, Kolkata and Chennai non-veg heavy; Bengaluru with a large vegetarian order share) are plausible but not verified at city level: NFHS and SRS data are state or district level. Household structure also constrains outcomes: a cook's repertoire, a no-onion-garlic or no-egg kitchen, and mixed-preference families mean the best output is often a shared base with per-person add-ons, an inference from background knowledge rather than a sourced finding.

## What bodies need: a heavy metabolic burden, strong anchors and many unverified rules

The population-level need profile is clear even where individual rules are not. ICMR-NIN 2024 attributes 56.4% of India's disease burden to unhealthy diets, caps added salt at 5 g/day, cites about 20-25 g/day sugar, limits cereals/millets to 45% of energy and pulses/beans/meat to 15%, advises minimising ultra-processed food and discourages protein supplements ([The South First](https://thesouthfirst.com/news/icmr-releases-dietary-guidelines-for-indians-after-13-years-says-no-to-protein-supplements); press-reported, primary PDF not read). ICMR-INDIAB (113,043 participants) finds diabetes at **11.4% (about 101M)** and prediabetes at **15.3% (about 136M)**, with metabolic disease more common in urban areas ([Down To Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/news/health/india-s-diabetes-epidemic-just-got-more-grim-lancet-report-reveals-why-89946)). Cohort evidence links high white-rice intake with diabetes in South Asians (intake above about 450 g/day cooked, in CURES and PURE; observational) ([NutraIngredients-Asia](https://www.nutraingredients-asia.com/Article/2020/10/20/White-rice-and-diabetes-South-Asians-with-high-intake-at-heightened-risk-large-prospective-study)), and a millet meta-analysis finds lower glycaemic response but is heterogeneous and led by a millet-promotion body ([Frontiers in Nutrition 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnut.2021.687428/pdf)). In urban south India mean salt intake was 8.5 g/day, and hypertension prevalence was 48.4% in the top salt quintile versus 16.6% in the lowest ([CURES-53](https://core.ac.uk/works/20044350)). Anaemia affects 57% of women aged 15-49 (NFHS-5) ([PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10860231/)); 51% of vegetarians in one study had B12 below 200 pg/ml versus 21% of non-vegetarians ([Naik et al.](https://nutritionj.biomedcentral.com/track/pdf/10.1186/1475-2891-12-136)); vitamin D deficiency is estimated at 70-90% (industry news source) ([BioSpectrum](https://www.biospectrumindia.com/news/98/14599/70-90-of-indians-are-vitamin-d-deficient-at-risk-of-having-diabetes-and-hypertension.html)). Protein is a gap in framing too: average intake is reported around 0.6 g/kg against an RDA near 0.8-1.0 g/kg, though the supporting survey is industry-linked ([The Week](https://www.theweek.in/magazine/health/more/2023/08/26/india-needs-a-more-targeted-approach-to-raise-protein-consumption.amp.html)).

Allergy and intolerance data justify asymmetric safety design. FSSAI requires declaration of eight allergen groups on packs, with "may contain" for cross-contact, but loose, restaurant and delivery food is largely uncovered ([SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2021/06/11/fssai/)). Coeliac prevalence is about 1.23% in North India versus 0.10% in the South ([Beyond Celiac](https://www.beyondceliac.org/research-news/geographic-variations-in-prevalence-of-celiac-disease-in-adults-in-india/)). Lactose intolerance estimates (66.6% South vs 27.4% North in an older study) are method- and decade-dependent, and the popular "60-65% of Indians" is weakly sourced ([The Week fact check](https://www.theweek.in/news/health/2025/01/15/fact-check-should-keralites-give-up-milk.html)). Fasting while on insulin or sulfonylureas carries hypoglycaemia risk and needs medical supervision ([BMJ Open Diabetes Res Care](https://drc.bmj.com/content/8/1/e001248)), which is why fasting-plan features must be gated for at-risk users. All remaining condition rules in the medical note (CKD potassium and phosphorus, warfarin vitamin K, levothyroxine timing, pregnancy avoidances, statin and metformin interactions, dyslipidaemia, PCOS, MASLD, thyroid, IBS and GERD, South Asian waist cut-offs) are explicitly "background, unverified" and appear in this report only as flags that route to "ask a doctor or pharmacist".

## How the factors interact: a causal map, with evidence tiers

The factors the brief names (foodie taste, physiology, medical, environment, upbringing, region, past) do not add; they interact in identifiable ways. Region and upbringing set the base cuisine, staple (rice vs wheat vs millet), fish or meat habit and spice tolerance; they also tilt risk, because the staple is the largest carbohydrate exposure and a rice-based South Asian pattern is where the white-rice evidence applies. Medical state then converts some preferences into constraints: diabetes pushes down portion size of rice and sweets, hypertension pushes down pickles, papad, namkeen and restaurant gravies, and coeliac disease converts wheat from preference into hard exclusion (more likely in the North). Environment adds a time-varying layer, and the evidence tiers are unequal. Strong: heat-season hydration (ORS, chaas, lemon water, water-rich fruit; per Health Ministry advisories, via news reports) ([Deccan Chronicle](https://www.deccanchronicle.com/lifestyle/citizens-advised-to-stay-hydrated-1865963)); short sleep raising hunger hormones and appetite ([PubMed 23199168](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23199168/)); monsoon waterborne-disease hygiene, with the "avoid leafy greens entirely" folk rule weaker than "wash and cook" ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/health/food-safety-tips-you-need-to-know-to-avoid-monsoon-related-diseases-124071100878_1.html)). Weak: Ayurvedic ritucharya as a system ([PMC3361919](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3361919/)) and "nutrition against pollution", which rests on observational, mechanistic and animal work ([medRxiv preprint](https://medrxiv.org/node/915200)); both can inform tone ("light, warm, fresh") but not promises.

Economics couples with season and hygiene. Tomato prices typically spike in July-August (Delhi Rs162/kg in July 2023) ([EastMojo](https://eastmojo.com/national/2023/07/07/tomato-prices-skyrocket-to-rs-162-per-kg-amid-short-supply/)), the same weeks when street food and cut fruit are riskiest, so the high-value rule set is "simple, hot, home-cooked, pulse-based, tomato-light". Crisil's January 2025 index shows broilers up 33% year on year (about half of non-veg thali cost) while the veg thali rose 2%, so protein substitution (egg, pulses, soy) is a price-driven alternative as well as a health one ([Crisil](https://intelligence.crisil.com/content/dam/crisilcom2-0/our-analysis/reports/crisil-intelligence/2025/02/roti-rice-rate-february-2025.pdf)). Time and kitchen constraints correlate with ultra-processed and late-night ordering: 44% of urban millennials reported skipping breakfast during COVID in an industry-sponsored study ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/lifestyle/food/story/44-urban-millennials-skipped-breakfast-during-covid-19-pandemic-study-307568-2021-09-24)), and quick-commerce late-night snack orders are a growing trade-reported pattern ([Restaurant India](https://www.restaurantindia.in/article/quick-commerce-vs-traditional-restaurants-how-consumer-behaviour-is-changing-in-india.16108)). No quantitative correlation matrix across these factors exists in the notes; the interactions above are mechanisms supported by individual studies, not estimated effect sizes.

How the evidence should be used in alternatives: the want-versus-need gap is best closed by preserving the craved sensory profile while removing the blocker. Illustrative ladders (design suggestions, not evidence): biryani to millet or brown-rice biryani with curd raita, or the same biryani with smaller portion and protein/vegetable first; late-night chicken burger to a boiled egg, curd or roasted chana (150-250 kcal protein-fibre snack, moderate evidence); vrat comfort food (sabudana khichdi) to a version with curd, paneer or makhana to cut glycaemic load; monsoon pakoda to home-fried or air-fried fresh batch rather than street chaat. For vegetarians, the post-workout and protein gap is closed with paneer, soy, hung curd, dal-rice combinations and sprouts before supplements, consistent with the ICMR-NIN stance; B12 is the exception, where food alone often fails and the engine should recommend a clinician test and never prescribe doses.

## What data to collect and how: methods ranked by effort, evidence and risk

Elicitation methods with the best evidence are visual and pairwise comparison quizzes. PlateClick bootstraps taste from adaptive pairwise images in 15 interactions or fewer ([KU profile](https://researchprofiles.ku.dk/da/publications/plateclick-bootstrapping-food-preferences-through-an-adaptive-vis/)); Yum-me combines visual comparisons with nutrition goals ([arXiv 1605.07722](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1605.07722)); and active learning chooses the next question by information gain ([arXiv 2309.00356](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2309.00356)). Transfer to Indian dishes is the likely failure mode (gravies of different dishes look alike), so cards should be curated Indian dishes with hand-built feature vectors (cuisine, main ingredient, spice, texture, cooking method, veg/non-veg) rather than image embeddings. For diet-quality baseline, the Diet Quality Questionnaire uses 29 yes/no foods-eaten-yesterday items in about 5 minutes across more than 85 countries ([PMC11331720](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11331720/)), and is the right model for a short Indian-adapted recall; Indian food-frequency questionnaires run 92-360 items and are valid only at group level ([PMC7071154](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7071154/); [PubMed 22705424](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22705424/)), so EatOS should borrow their food lists and portion units (katori, roti count) rather than the instruments. The Food Neophobia Scale is widely translated but its structure is unstable across cultures ([Frontiers 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2024.1356210/full)); two to four probe items suffice.

Behavioural signals complement stated ones: accepted, skipped and "ate this" events with a one-tap reason code (taste, effort, time, cost, missing ingredient, health, family) gathered passively inside the app; context from clock, calendar, weather, AQI and city; optional wearables (sleep, workout). Photos, receipts and SMS/UPI parsing are high-risk and should be off by default, opt-in per source and on-device only. Population-level methods inform priors rather than the individual model: NFHS/SRS/Pew for diet prevalence, HCES for spend, Swiggy/Zomato/Blinkit reports for ordering, Crisil and Agmarknet-type price series for cost, and IFCT 2017 (528 key foods, 151 components, six regions) for nutrient composition ([NIN IFCT 2017](https://nin.res.in/ebooks/IFCT2017.pdf)). For the "wish but cannot" signal no validated method exists; the proposed measure is the gap between stated "would eat" and revealed "did eat" per dish, plus a structured follow-up on the blocker.

## Recommendation-engine design: three layers, a transparent score and an asymmetric safety stack

The layer model is the heart of the design. Hard constraints are absolute filters applied before scoring: declared allergies and derivatives, declared coeliac disease, religious or ethical rules (Jain, no beef, no pork, satvik, fasting-day deny-lists), declared medical diet orders, pregnancy hazards, child choking hazards and feature gates for at-risk groups. Situational constraints (no kitchen in a PG or hostel, no fridge, minutes available, budget cap, canteen-only) are hard for the moment and expire or re-ask. Soft preferences (taste, cuisine affinity, spice, texture) and soft health goals (protein gap, fibre, sodium, sugar) are scored, never gating. Blocked aspirations are records `{item, blocker_type, blocker_detail, last_seen, attempts}`; the blocker taxonomy is cost, time, skill, equipment, availability, household veto, health advice, social setting, habit and taste mismatch with the healthier swap. The proposed score is `score = hardFilter × [w_taste·P(like) + w_habit·habit(dish, slot) + w_ctx·fit(time, effort, budget, weather, day-type) + w_health·gapFill + w_novel·explore] − repeatPenalty`, with weights moving from stated to revealed signals over about two to three weeks, and Thompson sampling or a decaying prior for exploration. This formula is a design proposal to A/B test; no source validates a fusion rule. Transparent linear scoring lets every card state its reason ("no onion-garlic, 10 minutes, 18 g protein"), which also supports on-device operation.

Safety rules should be asymmetric. A violation of a hard constraint must be impossible to emit; unknown allergen status of a restaurant or delivery item is shown as "unverified", never "safe"; the engine never infers or states a diagnosis (diabetes, PCOS, thyroid, anaemia, coeliac, lactose intolerance, allergy, CKD, pregnancy, eating disorder) from logs, photos or purchases; medication categories raise "ask your doctor or pharmacist" flags rather than computed advice; red-flag symptoms route to medical care. Behaviour-change evidence argues for modesty and for harm-avoidance. App-based dietary interventions raise fruit and vegetables by only **0.48 portions/day** (21 studies; 11,389 participants), mostly in high-income countries and with high dropout, and the techniques most associated with effect are goals and planning, feedback and monitoring, and personalisation ([PMC12487266](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12487266)). Calorie tracking is associated with eating-disorder symptoms: among 105 people with diagnosed eating disorders, about 75% used MyFitnessPal and 73% of those said it contributed ([Levinson et al. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5700836/)); these are retrospective, self-reported samples. EatOS should therefore be food-first not number-first: no calories by default, no streak shaming, no "good/bad" labels, no weight-loss default goal, a supportive-mode switch for restriction patterns detected on device, and three options by default plus "same as yesterday" and "surprise me". Evaluating success as "ate it and felt fine" is the honest outcome measure, since no health recommender in the diet-related review examined behaviour over time ([scoping review](https://nutrition-evidence.com/article/544703/diet-related-health-recommender-systems-for-patients-with-chronic-health-conditions-scoping-review)).

Privacy design follows from the architecture. With no account and on-device storage, data minimisation is the strongest story; DPDP Act 2023 requires consent that is free, specific, informed, unconditional, unambiguous and as easy to withdraw as to give, and treats under-18s as children needing verifiable parental consent with behavioural tracking barred ([KPMG](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmg/in/pdf/2023/08/decoding-the-digital-personal-data-protection-act-2023.pdf); [law.asia](https://law.asia/childrens-data-protection-dpdp-act/)). Secondary sources date the staged rule rollout with core obligations from 13 May 2027 ([Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/amp/digital/breaking-govt-notifies-final-dpdp-rules-sets-staggered-rollout-with-key-obligations-for-data-fiduciaries-84201.htm)); whether purely local processing falls outside the fiduciary duties is unconfirmed and needs legal review. Religion and health data revealed by diet (Jain, fasting, diabetes) should be treated as sensitive by design, with per-category consent, export and delete, and no inference of religion, caste or health for ads or insurers. Fairness requires auditing catalogue coverage by region and community and avoiding popularity priors that bury regional cuisines ([arXiv 2406.09496](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.09496v4)); nutrient values for mixed dishes and street food need ranges, not false precision.

## Implementation proposal

### Prioritised modules for the on-device TypeScript kernel

The ordering puts safety and constraint correctness first, then the scoring loop, then personalisation and context. Each module is pure TypeScript with typed inputs and outputs, no network dependency, deterministic given a seed, and unit-testable.

| Priority | Module | Responsibility | Key interfaces and notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| P0 | Profile store and consent ledger | Versioned, encrypted-at-rest local profile; per-category consent flags (diet, health, religion, body, passive sources); export, delete, pause "health mode" | `Profile`, `ConsentRecord`; no account; age band gate (<18 disables tracking features) |
| P0 | Dish and ingredient catalogue | Curated Indian dish set with ingredient lists, cuisine/region tags, spice, texture, method, veg/egg/non-veg, allergen flags, time and cost bands; nutrient ranges from IFCT-derived values | Starts with a few hundred dishes covering all regions; each dish carries an `allergenConfidence` (declared vs unverified); licensing of IFCT to be checked |
| P0 | Constraint engine | Hard filter: allergens, coeliac, religious and ethical rules, medical orders, situational limits; fasting templates with allow-list or deny-list modes | `evaluate(dish, profile, context) -> {allowed, reasons[]}`; allergen unknown returns `unverified`; fasting templates user-editable |
| P0 | Safety and gating layer | Feature gates (no fasting plans for insulin/SU users, pregnancy, minors, ED history); red-flag detection on device; supportive-mode switch; "ask a doctor" flags for medication categories; no diagnosis wording linter | Rule table with evidence-tier labels (guideline / observational / traditional); copy lint for banned phrases |
| P1 | Context builder | Clock/slot, day-type, month/season by city, festival and fasting calendar, optional weather, AQI, sleep and workout flags, price flags (tomato/onion) | Pluggable providers; works with just time and city; calendar data are templates pending verification |
| P1 | Preference model and onboarding | 10-15 adaptive pairwise cards over hand-built feature vectors, Bradley-Terry or Bayesian logistic update over 20-40 features, "never" as a separate hard tap, spice tolerance, 2-4 neophobia probes | Next-card choice by expected information gain plus diversity; under 2 minutes, all skippable |
| P1 | Scorer and explainer | Linear score per the formula above, repeat penalty, Thompson-sampled exploration, top-3 with one-line reasons | Weights in a versioned config; unit tests of constraint-first ordering |
| P1 | Event log and feedback loop | Accepted / skipped / ate-it events with one-tap reason codes; decay of stated prior toward revealed behaviour; per-dish stated-versus-revealed gap | Feeds both the scorer and the aspiration module |
| P2 | Aspiration and alternatives engine | Store blocked aspirations; generate the four-rung ladder (modified recipe, near-neighbour, same nutritional job, rescheduled) limited to three, each with the blocker it removes | Needs substitution rules and a sensory similarity metric (open; see assumptions) |
| P2 | Need and gap profile | Short DQQ-style Indian food-group recall, weekly food-group coverage, soft nudges on protein, fibre, sodium, sugar, iron pairing; no calories by default | Opt-in numbers; ranges not point values; evidence tier shown |
| P2 | Household and meal-source planner | Meal source (self, cook, family, outside), base-plus-variants planning for mixed households, cook-friendly recipe cards with regional-language support, batch and leftover logic | Shared base with per-person add-ons; cook instructions simple and translatable |
| P3 | Price and availability adaptor | Substitution suggestions on price spikes (tomato, onion, potato, broiler) and ingredient gaps, "use-first" leftovers | Needs a price feed or manual flag; offline fallback is seasonal defaults |
| P3 | Evaluation harness | Offline replay of synthetic personas, constraint-violation fuzzing (must be zero), A/B of weight shifts, success metric "ate it and felt fine" | Persona fixtures below serve as test cases |
| P3 | Optional cloud boundary | Opt-in sync, aggregate or LLM calls with identifier stripping; off by default | Deferred until legal review of DPDP status |

## 6. Assumptions, educated guesses and carried-over gaps

The evidence base is mostly search snippets and secondary or industry sources; none of the notes read primary documents in full. The following assumptions are made explicit so that the build team can verify or replace them.

**Evidence assumptions**
1. Swiggy-reported figures, trade-press summaries and the market-research numbers (for example the food-delivery market size) are used as directional indicators only; they are self-published or low-confidence and skew to online-active users.
2. ICMR-NIN 2024 numbers (5 g salt, 20-25 g sugar, 45% cereal energy, 15% pulses/beans/meat, 56.4% disease burden) come from press coverage; the primary NIN PDF was not read. One news source claims protein is 14% of energy against 6-9% current intake, and the notes treat this as unverified.
3. NFHS-5 figures are from an advocacy-site summary and the SRS 2014 figure is older; neither is city-level.
4. DPDP phase dates are from secondary sources; the gazette text is unchecked. Whether on-device-only processing is outside fiduciary obligations is unconfirmed.
5. Seasonal produce calendars, onion price seasonality (October-December spikes), caffeine and dehydration, and heat-index and AQI thresholds are general knowledge or unverified.

**Design assumptions (educated guesses, not evidence)**
6. The scoring formula, weight shifts over two to three weeks, a 10-15 card onboarding and information-gain card selection are proposals; no source validates a fusion rule, and Western food-image similarity is assumed not to transfer, so hand-built vectors are used.
7. Blocked-aspiration modelling is possibly novel; no published recommender models it. The four-rung alternative ladder and the blocker taxonomy are design inference.
8. Swap libraries (late-night, 20-minute dinners, hot day, rainy day, post-workout veg protein, travel, tiffin, stress eating) are practical inference with evidence tiers mostly moderate or weak; only the heat-hydration, sleep-appetite and monsoon-hygiene principles have solid support.
9. Fasting templates (Navratri, Shravan, Jain, Ekadashi, Ramzan, Lent) are editable defaults; primary-source rules for most were not retrieved and spice rules in particular conflict between sources.
10. Metro-level diet priors (Hyderabad, Kolkata, Chennai non-veg heavy; Bengaluru large vegetarian order share) are inferred; city-level prevalence data were not found.
11. Mixed-household base-plus-variant meals, cook-made versus self-cooked meal sources, thali and meal-timing norms, and the idea that comfort food is community-specific are background knowledge or inference, not sourced.

**Gaps carried over from the notes**
12. Missing for craving and psychology: no Indian peer-reviewed work on emotional or stress eating among metro adults, no upbringing or flavour-imprinting studies, no weather-craving evidence, no foodie-culture sources (reviews, social media, regional-restaurant trends).
13. Missing for want-but-cannot: no primary survey on suppressed wants with reasons; no evidence on housing-society veg-only rules, household food conflict or budget elasticity by city.
14. Missing for prevalence: out-of-home eating share and frequency by metro, urban-only NFHS vegetarian figures, nationally representative meal-skipping data (all breakfast figures are industry or college samples), joint versus nuclear household share by metro, cook employment rates.
15. Missing for medical: verified ADA, KDIGO, ESC, ESPEN, WHO and ACOG numbers; Indian glycaemic-load tables for staples; South Asian waist cut-offs; prevalence of hypertension (INDIAB about 35% is only background), dyslipidaemia, PCOS, thyroid, MASLD and food allergy; eating-disorder prevalence in India; medication-interaction source checks; keto, intermittent-fasting, A2 milk and seed-oil primary evidence.
16. Missing for methods: TFEQ, IES-2, AEBQ, HEI and Mediterranean-score details and licences; any validated Indian FFQ under 30 items; Hindi and regional-language validation of the neophobia scale; Yum-me and PlateClick quantitative results; recipe-level constraint-based or knowledge-based recommender literature; Zomato, Blinkit and Zepto behaviour reports; city-by-city dish rankings outside Hyderabad, Bengaluru and Mumbai.
17. Missing for data and law: licensed machine-readable IFCT 2017 terms for bundling; allergen-claim regulation on delivery platforms; SaMD and CDSCO classification for diet apps; ICMR ethics and telemedicine guidance.
18. Missing for substitution: the food_data_substitutions.md note was not available. Nutrient-equivalent swaps, flavour-network and sensory-similarity metrics, and ingredient-substitution prediction are therefore unresearched in this report, and the P2 alternatives engine should begin with hand-authored swap rules.

## Conclusion

The sharpest finding is structural: the three questions in the brief map onto three different data types with different failure costs. Wants are probabilistic and learnable from few interactions and behaviour; needs are partly population-level and anchored by guidelines but individually gated by declared conditions; blocked aspirations are neither, and exist only in the gap between stated and revealed choice plus a reason. Treating them in one scalar score hides the information that matters most, which is why a blocker-coded rejection and a stated-versus-revealed gap are the cheapest high-value signals in the whole design.

The second implication is about honesty of claims. The strongest evidence in the notes concerns the Indian context's risk landscape (diabetes, salt, anaemia, B12), while the weakest concerns exactly what EatOS must do well: model craving, family constraint and substitution. The first release should therefore win on constraint correctness, "unverified" labelling, and decision-load reduction, with the aspiration ladder shipped as hand-authored rules and treated as the main hypothesis to test with personas and real users, not as validated science.
