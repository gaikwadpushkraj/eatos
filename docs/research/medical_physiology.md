# Medical and physiological conditions shaping what an urban Indian adult should eat (safety/needs layer notes)

Scope note: general wellness guidance, not diagnosis. Research budget was limited (about 10 searches, search snippets only, few full-page fetches). Cited findings are what search results supported. Items marked "background, unverified" come from general knowledge and MUST be checked against the primary guideline before being coded as numeric rules.

## 1. For each condition: what to limit, favour, Indian foods that fit, numeric targets (official where available)

### Takeaway
Verified anchors: ICMR-NIN 2024 (salt <=5 g/day, free sugar about 20-25 g/day, fat <=30% energy, cereals/millets <=45% energy, pulses/beans/meat up to 15% energy); India's diabetes burden is 11.4% (101 M) with 15.3% prediabetes (136 M); white rice in large amounts is linked with diabetes in Indians; anaemia, B12 and vitamin D deficiency are very common. Most condition-specific numeric rules (CKD potassium, warfarin, levothyroxine timing, etc.) could not be verified in this session and are listed as background/gaps so the engine does not present them as sourced.

### Cited Findings
- ICMR-NIN 2024 has 17 guidelines; added salt capped at 5 g/day; sugar 20-25 g/day; fat up to 30% of energy; no more than 45% of energy from cereals and millets, up to 15% from pulses, beans and meat; advises against protein supplements; emphasises whole grains, millets, minimising ultra-processed food — [South First](https://thesouthfirst.com/news/icmr-releases-dietary-guidelines-for-indians-after-13-years-says-no-to-protein-supplements), [Daily Pioneer](https://www.dailypioneer.com/2024/columnists/key-takeaways-from-icmr---s-new-dietary-guidelines.html), [Nutrition Connect (source doc listing)](https://nutritionconnect.org/resource-center/dietary-guidelines-indians-2024-edition-icmr-nin). Press-reported figures; confirm against the NIN PDF.
- ICMR-INDIAB (Lancet Diabetes Endocrinol 2023, 113,043 participants): diabetes 11.4% (~101 M), prediabetes 15.3% (~136 M); Goa highest diabetes (26.4%), Uttar Pradesh lowest (4.8%); all metabolic NCDs except prediabetes more frequent in urban areas; diabetes plateauing in richer states, still rising elsewhere — [Cipla Med summary of ICMR-INDIAB](https://www.ciplamed.com/explore/key-trials/prevalence-of-diabetes-and-prediabetes-in-15-states-of-india-insights-from-the-icmr-indiab/), [Down To Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/news/health/india-s-diabetes-epidemic-just-got-more-grim-lancet-report-reveals-why-89946), [Manipal record](https://researcher.manipal.edu/en/publications/prevalence-of-diabetes-and-prediabetes-in-15-states-of-india-insights-from-the-icmr-indiab/)
- Glycaemic load/staples: CURES (Chennai) linked high white rice intake with prevalent and incident diabetes; pooled PURE data show South Asia (rice ~630 g/day) had the highest risk, ~60% excess risk; intake >450 g/day (cooked, 150 g per serving) associated with higher risk; advice to cut rice quantity and add dal/pulses — [Dr Mohan's summary](https://drmohans.com/backend/?p=6993), [NutraIngredients-Asia on PURE](https://www.nutraingredients-asia.com/Article/2020/10/20/White-rice-and-diabetes-South-Asians-with-high-intake-at-heightened-risk-large-prospective-study), [The Week](https://www.theweek.in/news/health/2020/09/17/new-study-confirms-white-rice-increases-risk-of-type-2-diabetes.amp.html)
- Millets: ICRISAT/NIN/Reading meta-analysis (Frontiers in Nutrition 2021; 65 studies in meta-analysis) found millets had lower GI than white rice, refined wheat; reported ~12-15% lower fasting/post-meal glucose in diabetics, HbA1c reduction in prediabetes; little millet data inconsistent — [Frontiers in Nutrition 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnut.2021.687428/pdf), [National Herald](https://www.nationalheraldindia.com/health/millet-based-diet-can-lower-risk-of-type-2-diabetes-study). Note: heterogeneous, small, mostly short trials, sponsor is a millet-promotion body.
- Hypertension/sodium: urban south India (CURES) mean salt 8.5 g/day vs WHO <5 g; top quintile of salt intake had hypertension prevalence 48.4% vs 16.6% in the lowest — [CURES-53 record](https://opact.sdu.edu.tr/vufind/SummonRecord/FETCH-LOGICAL-p124t-4e68568ec2b3950ef39d045823ee45e82b24ddafed381e6ca2bef8843340f933), [CORE](https://core.ac.uk/works/20044350)
- Anaemia: NFHS-5 (2019-21) anaemia in women 15-49 is 57% (up from 53% in NFHS-4) — [PMC analysis of NFHS-4/5](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10860231/), [The Wire Science](https://science.thewire.in/health/nfhs-5-anaemia-india-maharashtra-increasing-prevalence-government-conservative-attitude/)
- B12: in a cross-sectional study 51% of vegetarians had B12 <200 pg/ml vs 21% of non-vegetarians; in a north Indian tertiary-care series 86% of B12-deficient patients were strict vegetarians; B12 is absent from plant foods; deficiency common (~40%) in strict vegetarians and those >65 (secondary source) — [Naik et al., Nutrition Journal 2013](https://nutritionj.biomedcentral.com/track/pdf/10.1186/1475-2891-12-136), [PubMed 36119310](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36119310/), [Down To Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/amp/story/food/prescription-for-plant-based-diet-overlooks-vitamin-b12-deficiency-66193)
- Vitamin D: deficiency estimated 70-90% of Indians; 84% of T2D and 83% of hypertensive patients in a pan-India study were deficient (industry news source, treat as indicative) — [BioSpectrum](https://www.biospectrumindia.com/news/98/14599/70-90-of-indians-are-vitamin-d-deficient-at-risk-of-having-diabetes-and-hypertension.html)
- Celiac: prevalence ~1.23% North India, 0.87% North-East, 0.10% South (adults); ~1 in 90 north vs ~1 in 1000 south in an older multicentre study — [Beyond Celiac](https://www.beyondceliac.org/research-news/geographic-variations-in-prevalence-of-celiac-disease-in-adults-in-india/), [The Week](https://www.theweek.in/news/sci-tech/celiac-disease-north-india.html)
- Lactose intolerance: 66.6% of South Indian vs 27.4% of North Indian healthy volunteers in an older study; media figure "60-65% of Indians" is weakly sourced — [AGRIS record](https://agris.fao.org/search/en/records/65de3c744c5aef494fdb25d4), [The Week fact check](https://www.theweek.in/news/health/2025/01/15/fact-check-should-keralites-give-up-milk.html)
- Allergen labelling (FSSAI): mandatory declaration of cereals containing gluten, crustacea, milk, egg, fish, peanuts and tree nuts, soybeans, sulphite (>=10 mg/kg); separate "may contain" statement for cross-contact; oils from allergenic sources and raw agricultural commodities exempt — [SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2021/06/11/fssai/), [FoodSafetyWorks](https://foodsafetyworks.com/insights/allergen-labelling-changes-are-your-labels-updated). Gap: loose/restaurant/delivery food is largely not covered by pack labelling.
- Fasting and diabetes: intermittent fasting/Ramadan fasting can be safe under medical supervision; hypoglycaemia risk is main hazard with sulfonylureas and insulin; SGLT2 inhibitors need not be adjusted but should not be newly started right before fasting; pre-fast education and CGM/glucose monitoring recommended — [BMJ Open Diabetes Res Care](https://drc.bmj.com/content/8/1/e001248), [Cleveland Clinic J Med](https://www.ccjm.org/content/84/5/357), [Frontiers Endocrinol](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/endocrinology/articles/10.3389/fendo.2021.624423/pdf)

### Inferences
Rule-like engine logic derived from the above and flagged background knowledge (VERIFY before shipping numbers):
- Diabetes/prediabetes (soft-to-moderate): cap cooked white rice/refined atta per meal (about 1 katori/150 g cooked as default; evidence anchor is >450 g/day = risk); pair every carb with dal/legume, vegetables, curd or protein; favour whole/millet/mixed-flour rotis, dals, sprouts, non-starchy sabzi, nuts in small portions; limit mithai, sugary chai, juices, fried snacks (samosa, bhujia), refined-flour (maida) items; sweetened beverages should be near zero. Carbohydrate-percent targets for diabetes (ICMR-NIN's 45% energy cereals cap is the only sourced one). Background: South Asians develop diabetes at lower BMI/waist (thin-fat/high visceral fat phenotype, lower beta-cell reserve), so do not use BMI alone to judge "healthy" and use waist as risk marker (commonly cited cutoffs: men >=90 cm, women >=80 cm; unverified here).
- Hypertension: hard-ish limit for added salt 5 g/day (sourced); high-sodium Indian items to flag: pickles (achaar), papad, namkeen/chips, instant noodles, chutney powders, processed cheese, canned/packaged soups, restaurant gravies, dried fish/karuvadu. Favour potassium-rich foods (banana, coconut water, leafy greens, legumes) unless CKD/potassium-raising drugs (see below). DASH-style pattern is background knowledge.
- Dyslipidaemia/heart disease (background, unverified): limit ghee/vanaspati/palm oil/deep-fried reheated oil, trans fat (target <1% energy WHO), coconut-heavy gravies, red/organ meat excess; favour mixed oils, nuts, flax/mustard/fish (omega-3), oats, legumes, soluble fibre, vegetables. High lipoprotein(a) and TG in South Asians noted in literature, not verified here.
- PCOS (background): insulin-resistance-driven; same low-GL approach as prediabetes, weight-neutral framing; no supplement/diagnosis inference.
- Thyroid (background): do not restrict goitrogens (cabbage, cauliflower, soy) for normal cooking quantities; ensure iodised salt but within 5 g; levothyroxine timing rule below.
- Fatty liver (MASLD) (background): limit sugar-sweetened drinks, fructose, refined carbs, alcohol, fried food; favour fibre, coffee, legumes; weight loss 7-10% is the cited target (verify).
- Anaemia: favour iron-rich foods (ragi, bajra, jaggery in moderation, leafy greens, legumes, dried fruit, organ meat/fish/egg for non-vegetarians) plus vitamin C (amla, lemon, guava) at the meal; avoid tea/coffee within 1 h around iron-rich meals; B12 for vegetarians via dairy, eggs, fortified foods, otherwise supplementation requires clinician testing. Never recommend high-dose iron or B12 on the app's own authority.
- IBS/GERD/acidity: soft preferences, highly individual; GERD triggers (spicy, fried, large late meals, tea/coffee on empty stomach, carbonated drinks, mint, chocolate) are common but evidence for avoiding all is weak; low-FODMAP is a clinician-guided short-term elimination, not a default (background).
- Celiac: HARD constraint if self-reported diagnosed: exclude wheat (atta, maida, suji, dalia), barley, rye, and flag hidden gluten (semolina, seviyan, malt, some papads/soy sauce/masala blends/stock cubes, roasted-gram flour adulteration); naturally gluten-free staples: rice, jowar, bajra, ragi (check cross-contact), besan, dals. Oats need certified GF. Prevalence higher in North India.
- Lactose intolerance: SOFT unless medically confirmed; fermented dairy (curd, chaas, paneer) is often tolerated better than milk; ragi/sesame/leafy greens/fortified alternatives for calcium. Do not label users "lactose intolerant" from symptoms.
- Food allergy: HARD constraint on user declaration. Peanut, tree nut (cashew, almond, pistachio), shellfish/crustacea, fish, egg, milk, soy, sesame. India-specific hazards: groundnut oil and peanut in chutneys/poha/chaat/street food, cashew paste in restaurant gravies/mithai, ghee/milk in nearly all sweets, egg in naan/bakery/mayonnaise, coconut milk sometimes classed as tree nut. Cross-contact cannot be guaranteed for restaurant/delivery food; app should show "cannot verify" rather than "safe".
- Pregnancy/lactation (background): ICMR-NIN has extra energy/protein for pregnancy; folic acid supplementation before and early pregnancy and IFA tablets (government programme) are clinician-led. Avoid: raw/undercooked egg/meat/fish, unpasteurised milk/soft cheese, raw papaya, high-mercury fish, excess vitamin A (liver), alcohol; limit caffeine (about 200 mg/day commonly cited), herbal/ayurvedic preparations (e.g., high-dose fenugreek, ajwain-heavy remedies) without advice. Cultural "hot/cold" food avoidances (papaya, pineapple, sesame, eggs, dates in some communities) should be respected as preference but gently corrected where they cause nutritional restriction (e.g., egg/milk avoidance); raw papaya is the one with sound safety basis. Lactation: galactagogue foods (methi, garlic, gond laddoo, panjiri, jeera water) are cultural, low-risk if not sugar/ghee-excess; evidence weak.
- Children/adolescents: cannot apply adult diet/weight-loss/diabetes rules; ICMR-NIN has age-specific RDAs; adolescent girls' anaemia is a priority; no calorie restriction, no fasting, no keto, no supplement advice by app; limit ultra-processed/sugary drinks; hard block: whole nuts/peanuts under ~4-5 years (choking, background).
- Older adults: higher protein needs (about 1.0-1.2 g/kg/day commonly cited, ESPEN/PROT-AGE, not verified here; ICMR-NIN 2024 warns against protein supplements); soft/moist textures (khichdi, dalia, idli, curd, soups, mashed sabzi, egg preparations), dysphagia = clinician-led texture level, hard-block whole nuts/dry seeds where swallowing difficulty declared; B12/vitamin D/calcium concerns; hydration; avoid very low-salt unsupervised.
- CKD (background, verify with KDIGO/ISN/Indian Society of Nephrology): only declared diagnosis should trigger; potassium/phosphorus restriction applies by stage and labs, NOT for general users; high-K Indian foods: banana, coconut water, potato, tomato, orange, leafy greens (palak), dried fruit, lentils (soak/leach); high-P: processed foods with phosphate additives, colas, dairy, nuts; protein level and fluid per nephrologist. Avoid salt substitutes (KCl). Do not give targets without clinician input.
- Cancer treatment (background): individualised by oncologist; neutropenic/food-safety precautions (avoid raw/street food, unpeeled raw items), small frequent energy- and protein-dense meals, manage nausea (cold bland foods, ginger, dry toast/khichdi), avoid unproven "anti-cancer" diets/turmeric megadoses/alkaline or keto cures; interactions with herbal supplements.
- Post-illness recovery (background): khichdi (rice+moong dal), dalia, ORS/coconut water/nimbu pani/chaas for dehydration, banana, curd, soups, egg; resume protein gradually; with diarrhoea in infants/children, ORS plus zinc and continued feeding. Khichdi is soft and fine for most but high GL if rice-heavy for diabetics: add vegetables/more dal.
- Medication-food interactions (background, verify): metformin (take with meals; long-term B12 deficiency risk, relevant for vegetarians); levothyroxine (empty stomach, 30-60 min before breakfast; separate from calcium, iron, soy, high-fibre by about 4 h; coffee can reduce absorption); warfarin (keep vitamin K intake consistent: palak, methi, sarson, cabbage, coriander, not avoid; limit alcohol, cranberry, large herbal/turmeric/ginger/garlic supplements; fluctuating tea/rice-bran, mango, papaya intake reported); statins (grapefruit/large amounts of pomelo (some, notably simvastatin); alcohol); iron tablets (take with vitamin C, away from tea, coffee, milk, calcium; may darken stool, constipation); SGLT2 inhibitors (genital infection, dehydration, ketoacidosis risk with very low carb/fasting); sulfonylureas/insulin (hypoglycaemia with skipped meals/fasting); ACE inhibitors/spironolactone (high potassium); MAOIs (tyramine; rare). Treat as flags that prompt "ask pharmacist/doctor", not as engine-computed doses.
- Diabetic fasting (religious or intermittent): users on insulin or sulfonylureas, pregnant, with type 1, CKD, recent hypoglycaemia or unstable control should not be offered fasting plans by the engine; defer to doctor. Navratri/Ekadashi/Karva Chauth/Ramadan/Roza: typical fasting foods (sabudana, kuttu, singhara flour, potato, fried items, sweets, dates at iftar) are high GL; suggest those with protein (curd, paneer, nuts, makhana, milk), avoid breaking fast with large sugar load. Time-restricted eating 12-14 h eating window is gentler than 16:8+ (background).
- Eating disorders: do not provide calorie-counting/weight-loss, fasting, or restrictive-diet features to users who self-report ED history, are minors, or show red-flag patterns (very low targets, repeated extreme restriction, compensatory exercise language); show supportive messaging and clinician/helpline referral; avoid moralising food language, streak shaming, "clean/bad" labels. Evidence base on Indian ED prevalence not retrieved.

## 2. Hard safety constraints (never recommend) vs soft preferences

### Takeaway
Hard constraints should be limited to user-declared allergies, declared celiac disease, declared medical diet orders (e.g., CKD stage, dysphagia texture, warfarin vit K consistency), pregnancy hazards, child choking hazards, and fasting/restriction features for at-risk groups. Everything else (low GI, low salt, millets, plant-based, probiotic, GERD triggers) is soft/ranking.

### Cited Findings
- FSSAI recognises 8 allergen groups for mandatory declaration and "may contain" for cross-contact, but not for oils or raw commodities — [SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2021/06/11/fssai/)
- Fasting under medication (SU/insulin) carries hypoglycaemia risk requiring medical supervision — [BMJ Open Diabetes Res Care](https://drc.bmj.com/content/8/1/e001248)
- Celiac disease requires lifelong gluten avoidance (clinical standard; see celiac prevalence source) — [Beyond Celiac](https://www.beyondceliac.org/research-news/geographic-variations-in-prevalence-of-celiac-disease-in-adults-in-india/)

### Inferences
- HARD (exclude, show reason): declared allergen and derivatives (including hidden sources in gravies, oils where relevant to peanut/sesame risk); declared celiac -> gluten; declared pregnancy -> raw papaya, raw/undercooked egg/meat/fish, unpasteurised dairy, alcohol, high-dose herbal supplements; declared CKD with clinician limits -> excluded K/P items; dysphagia -> unsafe textures; child <5 -> whole nuts; declared warfarin -> no "eat more greens" nudges and no supplements; on insulin/sulfonylurea -> no fasting/skip-meal plans; ED history/minor -> no restriction/weight-loss features; infants <6 months -> only breast milk/medical advice; honey <1 year.
- MEDIUM (warn + require acknowledgment): diabetes with high-sugar items; hypertension with very salty items; GERD triggers; lactose symptoms.
- SOFT (rank): GI/GL, millets, fibre, salt reduction in healthy people, ultra-processed limits, plant-forward patterns, iron-vit C pairing.
- Uncertainty rule: if a restaurant/delivery item's allergen status is unknown, label "unverified" not "safe".

### Gaps
- No Indian allergy prevalence data retrieved; no guidance on how allergen-free claims on Zomato/Swiggy are regulated.

## 3. Data to collect (with consent) and what never to infer or diagnose

### Takeaway
Collect minimal, user-declared, optional, granular-consent data; treat health data as sensitive personal data under India's DPDP Act 2023 (verify details); never infer or state a diagnosis from food logs, purchases, or wearable data.

### Cited Findings
- No source retrieved on DPDP Act applicability or health-app consent practice in this session.

### Inferences
- Collect (opt-in, editable, deletable, purpose-stated, separate consent per category): diet type (veg/eggetarian/non-veg/Jain/vegan; religious fasting days); declared allergies with severity (anaphylaxis history) and intolerances; diagnosed conditions chosen from a checklist ("told by a doctor", with "prefer not to say" and "not sure"); pregnancy/lactation status; age band (minors: guardian consent); medications by category only (diabetes drugs, insulin, warfarin/blood thinners, thyroid, iron/B12, diuretic/ACEi, statin, immunosuppressive); texture/swallowing needs; region/cuisine and cooking oil; optional height/weight/waist; optional lab values (HbA1c, ferritin, B12, creatinine/eGFR) self-entered, never OCR-diagnosed without confirmation.
- Collection methods: onboarding screens with plain language (Hindi/regional options), progressive profiling, "why we ask", easy "pause/skip health mode", periodic re-confirmation (conditions and medications change), medical-disclaimer, human referral prompts.
- Never infer/diagnose: diabetes, prediabetes, PCOS, thyroid, anaemia, celiac, lactose intolerance, allergy, CKD, pregnancy, eating disorder, depression, from logs/photos/purchases/search; never state "you have..."; use "you may wish to talk to a doctor" only on explicit user-reported symptoms or red-flag patterns (e.g., repeated extreme restriction -> supportive message). Do not infer religion/caste/health from food choices for profiling, ads or insurers. Do not sell data.
- Emergency pathway: symptoms of anaphylaxis, severe hypoglycaemia, dehydration in children/elderly, pregnancy danger signs -> urge medical care, not diet tips.

### Gaps
- DPDP Act, ICMR ethics, CDSCO/SaMD classification for diet apps, and Telemedicine guidelines were not researched.

## 4. Where evidence is weak or contested

### Takeaway
Keep the engine honest: label evidence strength; avoid single-nutrient or single-food claims; treat millets, A2 milk, seed oils, keto, intermittent fasting, ghee and "superfoods" as low-to-moderate evidence preferences, not rules.

### Cited Findings
- Millets: pooled analysis supports lower GI and glycaemic improvement but is heterogeneous, short-term, small trials, led by a millet-promotion initiative; little millet inconsistent — [Frontiers in Nutrition 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnut.2021.687428/pdf)
- Intermittent fasting: "can be safe under supervision and may reduce weight, central adiposity and HbA1c" (hedged); main risk is hypoglycaemia with SU/insulin — [BMJ Open Diabetes Res Care](https://drc.bmj.com/content/8/1/e001248)
- White rice evidence is observational (cohort, PURE/CURES), so association not proof of causation — [NutraIngredients-Asia](https://www.nutraingredients-asia.com/Article/2020/10/20/White-rice-and-diabetes-South-Asians-with-high-intake-at-heightened-risk-large-prospective-study)
- Lactose intolerance figures vary by region, method and decade; the widely quoted "60-65% of Indians" is media-sourced — [The Week fact check](https://www.theweek.in/news/health/2025/01/15/fact-check-should-keralites-give-up-milk.html)
- ICMR-NIN explicitly discourages protein supplements and ultra-processed foods — [South First](https://thesouthfirst.com/news/icmr-releases-dietary-guidelines-for-indians-after-13-years-says-no-to-protein-supplements)

### Inferences
- Contested items (background, no source retrieved): keto (short-term weight/glucose gain, long-term LDL, adherence, SGLT2/insulin ketoacidosis risk, not for pregnancy/kids/CKD/ED; do not push); intermittent fasting (similar to continuous calorie restriction in trials; not for at-risk groups); A2 milk (no robust human evidence of health advantage beyond lactose-free/GI comfort claims in some people; do not recommend over regular milk); seed oils (online claims of harm not supported by strong RCT evidence; guideline stance favours mixed oils and reducing frying/reheating; ICMR-NIN 2024 oil guidance needs verifying); ghee (limited portions; evidence mixed); turmeric/ayurvedic/"detox" claims (low evidence; supplement interactions e.g., warfarin); low-carb vs low-GI for Indians (RCT evidence limited); glycaemic index measurement variability between labs and cooking method (cooling rice/resistant starch, parboiled vs raw rice) — individual glucose response varies (CGM personalisation evidence limited); vitamin D screening/supplementation for all (debated); thin-fat phenotype as dietary driver (observational). 
- Engine behaviour: show evidence level (guideline / observational / traditional); never claim cures; avoid fear-based language.

### Gaps
- No retrieved primary sources for keto, A2 milk, seed oils, or IF-in-Indians RCTs; Cochrane/BMJ/NEJM sources not fetched. Recommend a follow-up pass (A2: Cochrane/EFSA 2009 opinion; seed oils: AHA advisory; IF: NEJM 2022 Wilkinson/Sutton, BMJ 2022 umbrella review; keto: ADA/Diabetes Care consensus).
