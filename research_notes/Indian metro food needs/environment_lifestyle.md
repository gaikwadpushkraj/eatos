# Environmental, seasonal, situational and lifestyle factors affecting what an Indian metro resident should eat

Method note: ~13 searches with snippet-level reading (no full page fetches). Search tool is US-only and returned many secondary/news sources; primary documents (ICMR-NIN PDF, IMD, CPCB, FSSAI) were NOT read directly. Items under "Inferences" marked [general knowledge] are from background knowledge, not verified in this session; treat as unverified and flagged by evidence tier: STRONG / MODERATE / WEAK-FOLK.

## Q1. Which cheap contextual signals (time, weather, calendar, sleep, workout, city) map to which rules, and how strong is the evidence?

### Takeaway
Most useful cheap signals are: temperature/heat-index, rain/monsoon flag (month + city), clock time (late night), sleep hours, workout flag, calendar (festival/fast/month), AQI, and a price/season flag. Evidence is strong for heat hydration, short-sleep/night-shift appetite effects, monsoon food-safety hygiene, and UPF limitation; weak/folk for Ayurvedic ritucharya and antioxidant "detox" claims against pollution.

### Cited Findings
- Health Ministry heat advisory: drink water even if not thirsty; ORS, buttermilk, lemon water, fresh juices recommended; avoid alcohol, tea, coffee, carbonated drinks; eat water-rich fruit/veg (watermelon, muskmelon, cucumber, grapes); avoid sun noon-3 pm — [Deccan Chronicle](https://www.deccanchronicle.com/lifestyle/citizens-advised-to-stay-hydrated-1865963), [India Environment Portal](https://indiaenvironmentportal.org.in/news-clippings/health-ministry-issues-advisory-as-india-reels-under-heat-wave), [The Week 2026](https://www.theweek.in/news/health/2026/05/19/heatwave-advisory-india.amp.html) (news reports of the advisory; not the primary document). Evidence tier: STRONG for hydration/ORS in heat (public-health consensus); the "tea/coffee dehydrate" claim is weak (habitual caffeine at normal doses is not clearly dehydrating) [general knowledge].
- Heat stroke is a medical emergency (core temp >40 C); heat-related illness surveillance runs under NPCCHH/IHIP — [The Week](https://www.theweek.in/news/health/2026/05/19/heatwave-advisory-india.amp.html).
- Short sleep is associated with reduced leptin, elevated ghrelin, higher hunger/appetite, higher BMI; night workers show blunted post-meal ghrelin suppression and xenin rise vs day workers — [PubMed 23199168](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23199168/), [PMC9105437](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9105437/). Tier: STRONG (experimental + observational) for sleep-loss to appetite; MODERATE for specific shift-work food outcomes.
- Shift work, especially rotating, is associated with obesity, CVD, glucose intolerance — [PMC9105437](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9105437/). Tier: MODERATE-STRONG (observational).
- Late-night and non-traditional-hour orders are a growing share of quick-commerce/food delivery; snacks (AOV Rs150-400) ordered multiple times a week, with first-two-rows placement driving impulse buys; Bengaluru highest for late-night orders — [Restaurant India](https://www.restaurantindia.in/article/quick-commerce-vs-traditional-restaurants-how-consumer-behaviour-is-changing-in-india.16108), [Restaurant India 2](https://www.restaurantindia.in/article/how-quick-commerce-is-changing-restaurant-ordering-habits.16517), [Flipkart Minutes report](https://www.storyboard18.com/brand-makers/-79222.htm). Tier: industry/trade reporting (moderate for behavior, no health outcomes).
- Air pollution: Australian cohort (Hazelwood coal-mine fire PM2.5, n=282 exposed + 166 unexposed, 8.5 yrs later) found vitamins A, E, magnesium, zinc attenuated PM2.5-cough association and omega-3 attenuated PM2.5-COPD association — [medRxiv preprint](https://medrxiv.org/node/915200) (preprint, observational). Vitamin C protective effects shown in a mouse model — [UTS news](https://www.uts.edu.au/news/2025/12/vitamin-c-may-protect-against-air-pollution). Review: [Nutrients 2015 "Nutritional solutions..."](https://0-doi-org.brum.beds.ac.uk/10.3390/nu7125539). Tier: WEAK-MODERATE (mostly mechanistic/animal/observational; human RCTs are small, ozone-asthma focused).
- Ritucharya: claims of gut-microbiome/season links and "further clinical research needed to validate efficacy" — [Amrita overview](https://www.amrita.edu/?p=646356), [PMC3361919](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3361919/), [Ayurvaid blog](https://ayurvaid.com/blog/ritucharya-in-ayurveda-an-actual-evidence-lies-in-ayurveda/). Tier: WEAK/FOLK as a system; individual elements (eat light/warm in monsoon, hydrating foods in summer, seasonal produce) coincide with mainstream advice.

### Inferences
- Proposed signal-to-rule map (rule logic is mine; evidence tier in brackets):
  - Heat index high (>~35 C, or IMD heatwave alert): promote buttermilk/chaas, nimbu pani with pinch of salt, ORS, coconut water, curd rice, cucumber/watermelon/muskmelon raita; demote deep-fried/heavy-gravy dinners [STRONG for fluids; MODERATE for food choice].
  - Rain/monsoon flag (Jun-Sep, region-adjusted): promote freshly cooked, hot, home food; boiled/filtered water; thoroughly washed or cooked leafy greens; demote street chaat/pani puri/cut fruit/raw salad from outside [MODERATE-STRONG for hygiene].
  - Sleep <6 h last night: suggest protein+fibre breakfast, avoid sugary quick-fix; flag higher hunger expected [STRONG physiology; MODERATE for practical effect].
  - Clock 23:00-04:00 + hunger: light protein-carb options (see Q3) rather than heavy delivery [MODERATE].
  - Workout today: add protein target to next meal within a few hours [MODERATE; see Q3].
  - AQI high (CPCB "poor"+): nudge to fruit/veg, nuts/seeds, omega-3 sources, adequate vit C/E - framed as general diet quality, not "detox" [WEAK]; do not promise protection.
  - Festival/fast calendar: switch to festive/fasting-aware rules (e.g., sabudana, makhana, fruits; portion/sweet moderation) [Q4].
  - Price flag (tomato/onion spike): suggest substitutes (Q4) [STRONG as economics].
  - City matters mostly for produce calendar, humidity (Mumbai/Chennai/Kolkata) vs dry heat (Delhi/Jaipur), winter (Delhi/NCR) and AQI.
- Collectible without user effort: time, month, city, weather API, AQI API, calendar. Ask-once: veg/non-veg, kitchen equipment, budget. Optional: sleep/workout from wearables.

- No India-specific trial showing diet reduces pollution harm; no primary FSSAI monsoon guidance read.
- Effect of humidity/winter on appetite in Indians: no India-specific source found in this session.
- Caffeine-dehydration claim not independently verified.

## Q2. What practical, evidence-flagged swaps work per situation (late night, 20-min dinner, hot day, rainy day, post-workout veg protein, WFH, commute, night shift, stress eating, alcohol, travel, tiffin)?

### Takeaway
Swaps should lean on cheap, Indian-pantry foods (dal/besan/curd/paneer/sprouts/eggs/soy) and ICMR-NIN direction: more pulses/legumes/vegetables, fewer UPFs and less salt/sugar/oil, caution on protein supplements. Specific swap lists are largely practical inference; supporting evidence is cited where available.

### Cited Findings
- ICMR-NIN Dietary Guidelines for Indians 2024: 17 guidelines; not more than 45% of energy from cereals/millets, up to 15% from pulses, beans and meat; minimise ultra-processed foods; fortification doesn't make UPF healthy; moderate salt, fat, sugar; physical activity — [South First](https://thesouthfirst.com/news/icmr-releases-dietary-guidelines-for-indians-after-13-years-says-no-to-protein-supplements), [GKToday](https://www.gktoday.in/icmr-releases-new-dietary-guidelines/), [ETV Bharat](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/!health/avoid-ultra-processed-fat-saturated-food-icmr-enn24052903811), [Nutrition Connect](https://nutritionconnect.org/resource-center/dietary-guidelines-indians-2024-edition-icmr-nin). One source says protein is 14% of energy vs current 6-9% — [South First](https://thesouthfirst.com/news/icmr-releases-dietary-guidelines-for-indians-after-13-years-says-no-to-protein-supplements) (this is a news paraphrase; the figure should be verified in the primary PDF — typical intake share is reported elsewhere ~10-12%).
- ICMR-NIN guidelines discourage protein supplements; a study found inaccurate protein labelling in 70% of 36 popular supplements tested in India — [South First](https://thesouthfirst.com/news/icmr-releases-dietary-guidelines-for-indians-after-13-years-says-no-to-protein-supplements). Tier: MODERATE (guideline stance; labelling study via news).
- Protein gap: RDA ~0.8-1.0 g/kg (~50-60 g/day); average intake reported ~0.6 g/kg; IMRB survey said 73% of urban rich "protein deficient", 93% unaware of requirement; India avg ~47 g/person/day vs ~68 g global — [Morung Express](https://morungexpress.com/increase-protein-intake-to-stay-healthy), [The Week](https://www.theweek.in/magazine/health/more/2023/08/26/india-needs-a-more-targeted-approach-to-raise-protein-consumption.amp.html), [ORF](https://www.orfonline.org/expert-speak/indias-protein-deficiency-and-the-need-to-address-the-problem), [BJN "Protein intakes in India"](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/british-journal-of-nutrition/article/protein-intakes-in-india/E4D9E34413FA06FF57844888FC6084C3). Caveats: IMRB figure comes via industry-linked survey (potential bias); quality/digestibility of cereal-pulse protein matters. Tier: MODERATE.
- Monsoon food safety: waterborne diseases (cholera, typhoid, hepatitis A, diarrhoea) peak; advice to drink boiled/purified water, avoid pani puri/chaat/pakoras/cut fruit from street sellers, wash leafy vegetables thoroughly (they carry mud, insects, microbes) — [Apollo](https://apollohospitals.com/diseases-and-conditions/water-borne-diseases-to-watch-out-for-this-monsoon), [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/health/food-safety-tips-you-need-to-know-to-avoid-monsoon-related-diseases-124071100878_1.html), [ETV Bharat](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/health/foods-avoid-monsoon-season-gastro-food-borne-diseases-enn26072802210). Hospital/media sources; statistics like "70% outbreaks" and "3.4 million" are unverified secondary figures. Tier: MODERATE-STRONG for hygiene principle; the "avoid leafy greens entirely in monsoon" folk rule is WEAK (washing/cooking suffices).
- Night-shift/sleep debt: see Q1 hormone findings — [PubMed 23199168](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23199168/).
- Heat drinks: ORS, buttermilk, lemon water, lassi — [Deccan Chronicle](https://www.deccanchronicle.com/lifestyle/citizens-advised-to-stay-hydrated-1865963).

### Inferences
Swap library (practical; evidence mostly [general knowledge]/inference unless cited above):
- Late-night hunger (post-11 pm): small portion (150-250 kcal) of protein+fibre: warm milk/haldi doodh, a bowl of curd, roasted chana/makhana, a boiled egg, a banana with peanut butter; avoid Maggi/biryani/ice-cream delivery. Sleep-loss link to hunger is STRONG; specific snack advice is MODERATE/inference.
- 20-minute dinner on a pressure cooker/induction/no oven: moong dal khichdi with veggies + curd; besan/moong chilla + curd; egg bhurji + roti; paneer/tofu bhurji; sprouts/chana pulao; dal-tadka + pre-made atta rotis. Pressure cooker cuts dal time ~10-15 min [general knowledge].
- Hot day: chaas, jaljeera, aam panna, sattu drink, curd rice, kheera raita, watermelon; moderate salt+fluids if sweating heavily; avoid heavy fried/non-veg late [fluids STRONG; foods MODERATE]. Sattu is folk-plus-nutrition plausibility (protein/fibre) [WEAK-MODERATE].
- Rainy day: hot soups, dal-rice, khichdi, ginger-tulsi tea (folk comfort, WEAK for immunity claims); cravings for pakoras: prefer home-cooked, fresh oil, air-fryer/bake option; avoid street cut fruit and outside chutneys [hygiene MODERATE].
- Winter (Delhi/North): seasonal greens (sarson, bathua, methi, palak), carrots, peanuts, jaggery/til in moderation, vitamin D/sun exposure reminders (vit D deficiency is widespread in urban Indians [general knowledge]) [moderate].
- Post-workout vegetarian protein: paneer/tofu/soy chunks, Greek-style curd/hung curd, dal+rice/roti combos, sprouts, chana, sattu, milk, peanuts; eggs if ovo-veg. Target ~20-30 g protein per meal [general knowledge; MODERATE]. Whole foods first given ICMR stance on supplements; if whey used, pick third-party-tested [inference].
- WFH: scheduled meals, avoid snacking at desk; keep fruit/nuts/roasted chana visible; avoid UPF snack stocking via quick commerce [WEAK-MODERATE].
- Long commute/late work: batch-cook dal/sabzi base on Sunday; tiffin with protein; avoid skipping lunch then heavy late dinner [MODERATE/inference].
- Night shift: largest meal before shift, light protein-rich small meals during, avoid heavy meal at 3-4 am, limit caffeine late in shift [MODERATE; general knowledge].
- Stress eating: swap packaged namkeen/biscuits for makhana, roasted chana, fruit, nuts; delay 10 minutes; ordering friction (don't open delivery app) [behavioral, WEAK-MODERATE].
- Alcohol, travel, festivals: see Q4. Rule examples: water/ORS alongside alcohol, eat protein before drinking, avoid mixing with fried salty snacks only; travel: carry nuts/roasted chana/ORS sachets, drink sealed/boiled water, avoid raw salad/ice from unknown places [general knowledge; MODERATE].

### Gaps
- Primary ICMR-NIN PDF not read: exact limits (salt, sugar, oil, protein per kg, meal-pattern advice) not verified.
- No India-specific trial for post-workout vegetarian protein, late-night snack outcomes, or WFH eating; guidance is extrapolated.
- Tiffin culture (Mumbai dabbawalas etc.) nutrition evidence: not found.

## Q3. Budget, price inflation, delivery vs home cooking, UPF growth, food waste, kitchen constraints and water quality — what patterns and rules apply?

### Takeaway
Veg prices (tomato/onion/potato) are strongly seasonal with a July-August tomato spike; Crisil's thali index gives a trackable cost signal. UPF consumption and quick commerce are growing quickly; household food waste is meaningful. Rules: substitute and shift when prices spike, cook-at-home batch strategies, limit UPF.

### Cited Findings
- Tomato prices typically spike July-August (monsoon harvest/transport disruption, lean production); examples: Delhi Rs73-85/kg in late July (2025), Rs162/kg in July 2023, Rs70-90 in 2024; July 2023 food inflation 11.51%, vegetables 37.43% y/y — [Asia News Network](https://asianews.network/?p=110592), [All India Radio/NewsOnAir](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/tomato-prices-spike-due-to-rain-disruptions-not-supply-shortage-govt), [EastMojo](https://eastmojo.com/national/2023/07/07/tomato-prices-skyrocket-to-rs-162-per-kg-amid-short-supply/), [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/economy/analysis/monsoon-induced-supply-disruptions-hold-key-to-vegetable-inflation-ahead-125081201817_1.html), [Sathee/IITK](https://sathee.iitk.ac.in/gk/current-affair/07-08-2024/business/tomato-prices-soar-to-7090-a-kg-amidst-heatwave-and-heavy-rain). Tier: STRONG for pattern (repeated years).
- Crisil Roti Rice Rate: Jan 2025 veg thali +2% y/y, non-veg thali ~+17%; potato +35% y/y to Rs31/kg; pulses +7%; veg oil +17% (import duty); LPG -11% to Rs803/14.2 kg (Delhi); broilers +33% (about 50% of non-veg thali cost); tomato -34% m/m and onion -21% m/m in Jan 2025 on rabi arrivals — [Crisil Feb 2025 report](https://intelligence.crisil.com/content/dam/crisilcom2-0/our-analysis/reports/crisil-intelligence/2025/02/roti-rice-rate-february-2025.pdf), [Crisil June 2025](https://intelligence.crisil.com/content/crisilcom/en/home/our-analysis/reports/2025/06/roti-rice-rate-june-2025.html); also tomato/onion/LPG pushed thali costs up in a June report — [Nuffoods Spectrum](https://nuffoodsspectrum.asia/news/9/6391/higher-tomato-onion-and-lpg-prices-push-up-indias-home-cooked-thali-costs-in-june-crisil.html). A July 2026 edition exists (not read) — [Crisil July 2026](https://intelligence.crisil.com/content/dam/crisil-intelligence/what-we-think/reports/2026/07/roti-rice-rate-july2026.pdf).
- UPF growth: India UPF sector ~13.4% CAGR 2011-2021 (Euromonitor-based); market value US$0.9 bn (2006) to US$38 bn (2019); per capita processed food sales US$31.3 (2012) to US$57.7 (2018); sweet biscuits 43% share in 2021 — [WHO SEARO/ICRIER](https://www.who.int/southeastasia/publications/i/item/9789290210672), [ICRIER phase 2](https://icrier.org/research/the-growth-of-ultra-processed-foods-in-india-an-analysis-of-trends-issues-and-policy-recommendations-phase-2/). Tier: MODERATE-STRONG (industry data, peer-reviewed analyses). ICMR-NIN advises minimizing UPF — [ETV Bharat](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/!health/avoid-ultra-processed-fat-saturated-food-icmr-enn24052903811). FSSAI reportedly to adopt ICMR norms for defining high fat-sugar-salt foods on FOP labels — [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/health/fssai-to-adopt-icmr-nutrition-norms-for-defining-food-high-in-fat-sugar-salt-on-front-of-pack-labels/2657660/).
- Quick commerce: 10-20 min delivery (Blinkit, Zepto, Instamart) extends to food; snack impulse purchases and late-night orders — [BNN Bloomberg](https://bnnbloomberg.ca/business/international/2024/12/20/disruptive-10-minute-deliveries-spread-to-indias-food-business), [Restaurant India](https://www.restaurantindia.in/article/how-quick-commerce-is-changing-restaurant-ordering-habits.16517).
- Food waste: UNEP Food Waste Index 2024 puts India household waste at ~55 kg per capita per year (~78-80 million tonnes total household-level estimate cited by secondary sources) — [Shankar IAS summary](https://www.shankariasparliament.com/current-affairs/mains-analysis/food-wastage-1), [National Herald](https://www.nationalheraldindia.com/amp/story/international/un-report-exposes-global-food-waste-crisis-as-india-grapples-with-alarming-levels), [Drishti](https://www.drishtiias.com/daily-updates/daily-news-analysis/indias-food-waste-conundrum/print_manually). Secondary sources; verify with UNEP primary (note total tonnes may include another definition). Tier: MODERATE.
- Water quality: drink boiled/purified water in monsoon — [Apollo](https://apollohospitals.com/diseases-and-conditions/water-borne-diseases-to-watch-out-for-this-monsoon).

### Inferences
- Price rules: if tomato >~Rs60-80/kg (or a +50% m/m flag), suggest tomato-light options (curd/onion-based gravies, tamarind/kokum bases, dal-tadka without tomato, frozen/puree), buy seasonal alternatives; if onion spikes, use cabbage/capsicum/spring onion; if potato high, swap with sweet potato/seasonal veg; if broiler spikes, shift to eggs/pulses/soy. Rule logic is inference from the cited price patterns.
- July-August is a double hit: peak price AND peak hygiene risk, so plan simple home-cooked pulse-based meals.
- Cook-at-home vs delivery: delivery tends to cost more and carry more salt/oil/refined carbs [general knowledge; no cost study cited here]; suggest "hybrid": cook dal/rice, outsource only a sabzi. 
- Kitchen constraints: one-pot pressure-cooker meals (khichdi, pulao, dal, rajma, chole, steamed idli), induction-friendly pans, air-fryer as oven substitute; no-cook meals (curd + sprouts salad, poha, upma, overnight oats, sattu) on hot days or low-energy evenings [practical inference].
- Food waste: use leftover roti/rice (rice pulao, roti upma/noodles), freeze dal/gravy bases, buy small amounts of perishables via quick commerce only when planned; suggest "use-first" ingredient logic in recommender [inference].
- Water quality: RO/boiling/filter; avoid ice outside in monsoon; hot beverages safest [general knowledge].

- No cost comparison of cooking vs delivery with sourced numbers.
- Water quality (BIS/CPCB/Jal Shakti metro data) not researched.
- Home-cooked thali Crisil monthly values for 2026 not read.

## Q4. Seasonal produce calendar, festivals, social eating, alcohol, travel, tiffin: what applies by month and region?

### Takeaway
Seasonal produce calendar below is largely [general knowledge] and has not been verified against a source in this session; only the monsoon/tomato/winter rabi arrivals are cited. Festival/social eating and alcohol rules are mostly general guidance.

### Cited Findings
- Tomato: lean production July-August; staggered summer-crop arrivals from south moderate spikes; fresh rabi arrivals cut tomato prices in Jan (-34% m/m in Jan 2025) — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/economy/analysis/monsoon-induced-supply-disruptions-hold-key-to-vegetable-inflation-ahead-125081201817_1.html), [Crisil Feb 2025](https://intelligence.crisil.com/content/dam/crisilcom2-0/our-analysis/reports/crisil-intelligence/2025/02/roti-rice-rate-february-2025.pdf).
- Heat-season water-rich produce (watermelon, muskmelon, cucumber, grapes) recommended — [Health Ministry advisory reports](https://indiaenvironmentportal.org.in/news-clippings/health-ministry-issues-advisory-as-india-reels-under-heat-wave).
- Leafy vegetables need thorough washing in the rains — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/health/food-safety-tips-you-need-to-know-to-avoid-monsoon-related-diseases-124071100878_1.html).
- ICMR-NIN guidance on moderation of salt/fat/sugar and UPF applies to festival foods — [GKToday](https://www.gktoday.in/icmr-releases-new-dietary-guidelines/).

### Inferences
Seasonal calendar [general knowledge, UNVERIFIED in this session; WEAK as a source level, high practical plausibility]:
- Jan-Feb (winter): carrot, peas, cauliflower, cabbage, spinach, methi, sarson, bathua, radish, turnip, beetroot, oranges/kinnow, guava, strawberries (north), sugarcane/jaggery, amla; fresh green chana, peanuts.
- Mar-Apr (spring/early summer): cucumber, tinda, bottle gourd, raw mango begins, watermelon starts, lemon, coconut; wheat harvest.
- May-Jun (peak heat): mango, watermelon, muskmelon, litchi (Jun), jamun, falsa, cucumber, bottle gourd, ridge gourd, raw mango (aam panna), mint, curd/chaas season.
- Jul-Aug (monsoon): bhutta (corn), plum, pear, jamun, pomegranate begins, gourds (lauki, tori, karela, parwal), colocasia; tomato scarce/expensive; leafy greens risky/needs care; in Maharashtra, Kerala etc. local rainy-season veg (e.g., ran bhajya, wild greens).
- Sep-Oct: apple (Himalayan), pomegranate, custard apple, guava, pumpkin, banana year-round; festival sweets season begins; cooler weather.
- Nov-Dec: winter veg start, green peas, cauliflower, oranges, singhara (water chestnut), sweet potato, amla, new-crop rice/jaggery; onion prices often elevated Oct-Dec [general knowledge; unverified].
- Regional: South — coconut/curry leaf/drumstick/raw banana year-round; East/Kolkata — fish and winter nolen gur; West — Mumbai monsoon bhajiya culture; North — winter greens and makki/sarson; Hyderabad/Chennai — humid heat much of the year so hydration relevant most months.
- Festival/social eating rules: Navratri/Ekadashi/Ramzan/Karva Chauth fasts: hydration, avoid long gaps then heavy fried breaking of fast (break with fruit/dates, water, then meal) [MODERATE; general]. Diwali/Eid/Holi: pre-plan one sweet portion, protein/fibre first, alcohol with water [WEAK-MODERATE].
- Alcohol: adds empty energy, impairs sleep and hydration; pair with protein, water, avoid late-night heavy delivery after drinks [MODERATE; general knowledge].
- Travel: carry shelf-stable protein; choose cooked, hot food; sealed water; avoid raw salads and ice in unknown venues (traveller's diarrhoea evidence STRONG [general knowledge]).
- Tiffin: reheatable proteins (dal, rajma, paneer, egg) plus roti/rice plus veg plus curd; limit fried/leaky oils; pack ice pack in heat to avoid spoilage [inference].
