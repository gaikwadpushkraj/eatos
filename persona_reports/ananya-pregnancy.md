## Verdict
- NOT safe to ship for a pregnant user: the raw-sprouts salad is offered (Ask "light lunch"), and the wish list suggests keeping raw papaya / sushi / unpasteurised cheese "for a weekend meal".
- Pregnancy hazard check is a substring match on ingredient names ("raw sprouts", "raw papaya") so real dishes slip past, and the wish list says "Nothing is in the way" for beer, kombucha, unripe pineapple.
- Tone, fasting refusal and safe mode are warm and non-medical; the recommendation engine just does not yet use what it knows about nausea, time of day or her pantry.

## What delighted her
- Today starts with Ragi dosa "A good iron source. A taste you grew up with." Warm, her cuisine, anaemia-aware, no lecture.
- The wish list "Find alternatives" tone is kind and non-alarmist: no red warnings, no fear words, no medical claims.
- Fasting refusal is brief and clinician-deferring; the plan is never altered. Nothing in the app plans fasting or calorie restriction (floor 1200 kcal explained).
- Safe mode quickly gives sip-size water tasks and "Gentle breakfast".
- Pregnancy-hazard dishes with listed hazards (egg curry, mayonnaise-like items, fish, salmon) never surfaced in 28 Ask runs; no non-veg shown. No console errors.
- Profile says health data stays on device; "Hide numbers" exists. Mobile layout clean (no horizontal scroll; big tap targets in screenshots).

## Missing for her
- A nausea/first-trimester mode (small frequent meals, cold/no-cook/low-odour dishes, ginger-lemon, dry crackers, curd rice, coconut water) that varies rather than repeating Ragi malt.
- Pregnancy hazard list that covers wishes and raw preparations (sprouts, raw papaya, unripe pineapple, unpasteurised dairy, street/pani puri, alcohol, caffeine, kombucha, high-mercury fish) with a gentle "better skipped for now" and swaps.
- Her actual worry about caffeine: no tea/coffee items or wording at all.
- Hot/cold ("heating/cooling") family-advice language: the app tags `cooling` on chaas/kachumber, but nothing explains or neutralises the conflicting advice; a one-line "no single food is hot or cold for your pregnancy; ask your clinician about specific foods" would help (not medical advice).
- Cook / family sharing: nothing for a cook who visits twice a day (a shareable "today's menu, with pregnancy notes" card, hygiene reminders, boiled-water prompts) or for ordering in (delivery menus are only in Integrations).
- The clinician line for supplements (finding 10), and one-tap hide-numbers for pregnancy.
